    How does this work?

    The agent is a tabular Q-learner written in C++17. It starts knowing nothing but the legal moves, plays five million hands against itself, and keeps a table of how much each move is worth in each situation. Those numbers are the Q-values in the panel: the expected result of that move, in bets. The page runs the same C++ engine and the same trained table, compiled to WebAssembly, so nothing is sent anywhere.

    The "cost" of a decision is the gap between the AI's value for its move and for yours. It's the AI's estimate, not the truth. Where the AI and textbook basic strategy disagree, the panel says so; those are the handful of close calls it still gets wrong.

    Rules: six decks, dealer stands on soft 17, blackjack pays 3:2, double on any two cards, one split, split aces get one card, no double after split, no surrender.

    Play money only, for learning; no real-money gambling. · Source, results and write-ups on GitHub
